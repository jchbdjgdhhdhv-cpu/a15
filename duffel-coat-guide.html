The Duffel Coat

Hood, toggles, rope loops and heavy wool: the duffel coat has barely changed in over a century, and for good reason.

By the Duffels editors · a six minute read

I.Where the name comes from

The coat takes its name from Duffel, a Belgian town known for a thick, napped woollen cloth. The design that followed, hooded and toggle-fastened, was adopted by navies because it could be closed quickly with cold, gloved hands.

II.The defining features

  • Toggles: usually four, of horn, wood or resin, fastened through rope or leather loops.
  • Hood: generous enough to go over a hat.
  • Yoke: a shoulder panel that adds weather protection.
  • Patch pockets: large, simple and often flapped.

III.The cloth matters most

A true duffel is cut from dense, heavy wool, often melton or a double-faced cloth. Heavier cloth (600 to 800 grams per square metre) blocks wind and drapes beautifully; lighter cloths are easier to wear in mild cities.

IV.Toggles and loops

Horn toggles are traditional and hard-wearing; wood is light and warm in tone. Rope loops look classic and casual, leather loops dressier. Check the loops are stitched firmly, as they take all the strain of fastening.

V.How to wear it

The duffel is relaxed by nature. Wear it over knitwear with jeans or chinos, or over a jacket for a smarter look. Navy and camel go with almost everything; charcoal suits city wear; brighter shades add cheer to winter.
